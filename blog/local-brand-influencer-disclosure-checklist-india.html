Local Brand × Creator Collaborations: An India Disclosure Checklist

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 21 October 2026

Disclosure begins with the relationship

The ASCI Influencer Advertising Guidelines require a disclosure when there is a material connection between an advertiser and an influencer. The official guidance makes clear that this is not limited to cash: free or discounted products, services or perks can also create the connection. Before scripting, write down what each side is giving and receiving. If there is no material connection and a creator independently discusses something they bought and liked, ASCI distinguishes that situation from advertising. Keep evidence of the actual arrangement instead of deciding based on how promotional the final reel feels.

Choose a permitted label before editing

ASCI’s current guidance lists disclosure labels and explains how they should identify advertising clearly. Select the applicable label during the brief stage and place it in the draft from the first edit. Do not rely on a vague thank-you, a brand tag or the hope that viewers will infer a collaboration. The label should be easy to notice and understand. Platform tools can support disclosure, but the brand and creator should still review the published result rather than assuming a setting remained visible after export or cross-posting.

Match disclosure to video and audio

For a short-form video, the disclosure must survive the real viewing experience: small screens, muted playback, captions, cuts and reposts. Put a readable disclosure on screen early and keep it visible for long enough to register. If the promotional claim is spoken, include an audible disclosure where the applicable guidance requires it. Check contrast against every background and keep important text away from interface buttons. Review the exported file on a phone, not only on an editing monitor.

Separate supplied facts from creator opinion

Create a claim sheet with three columns: the exact statement, the evidence supplied by the brand and the approved wording. Prices, availability, ingredients, awards, rankings, performance and results need current support. A creator can describe a genuine experience, but should not convert a personal impression into an objective guarantee. Avoid best, number one, permanent or risk-free unless the brand has adequate evidence and the claim is legally appropriate. When facts may change, record the verification date.

Give local businesses a compact approval pack

A useful handover contains the final script, on-screen text, caption, disclosure label, tagged account, destination link and publication date. Add a contact who can answer fact questions quickly. The creator should identify which changes affect accuracy or disclosure and which are stylistic. Keep one approved export and one approved caption so last-minute revisions do not create mismatched claims. This small archive also helps when a post is reused in ads, stories or other formats.

Do not hide uncertainty in a beautiful reel

If the team cannot verify an opening time, price, ingredient, origin claim or customer result, remove it or label the uncertainty honestly. Never invent a visit, tasting, interview or testimonial. Use the brand’s own verified footage with permission, commission new footage or choose a clearly labelled illustration when documentary visuals are unavailable. A polished reel can still be misleading if the scene, speaker or result is presented as something it is not.

Run a five-minute final check

Before publishing, ask five questions: Is the material connection documented? Is the ad label clear in the post itself? Can every factual claim be supported? Are music, footage, logos and customer faces authorised? Does the final caption match the final video? Then save a screenshot or copy of the live post. This article is an editorial workflow, not legal advice; teams should consult ASCI’s current official material and obtain professional advice for regulated products or uncertain claims. Transparent collaboration protects the audience and strengthens trust in local creators and businesses.
