Buying Khadi in Ahmedabad: A Practical Fabric and Care Checklist

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 16 October 2026

Begin with the making process

A textured fabric and a familiar label are not enough to explain how a cloth was made. A Ministry of MSME release published through the Press Information Bureau on 9 October 2026 describes khadi as hand-spun and hand-woven, and identifies hand spinning as the key difference from handloom more broadly. That gives a buyer a better starting question than whether a garment merely looks traditional: ask the seller what they know about the yarn, the weaving and the institution or maker supplying it.

Separate fibre, process and finish

Write down three different questions: what is the fibre composition, how was the yarn and cloth produced, and what finishing or dyeing has been applied? One answer does not establish the others. A cotton label describes fibre, while a process claim needs its own explanation. Ask the seller to show the product label and any documentation they rely on for the khadi description. If the answer is unclear, record that uncertainty instead of turning a soft feel or visible irregularity into proof of authenticity.

Buy for a real use in your wardrobe

Think about how you will wear the cloth before choosing the most eye-catching piece. For a ready-made garment, check shoulder movement, sleeve comfort, length and whether you can sit easily. For fabric by the metre, discuss your intended garment with the tailor before deciding the quantity. Ask about width and any expected shrinkage so the purchase fits the actual pattern. A cloth you use repeatedly can be a more thoughtful local purchase than a symbolic garment that never leaves the cupboard.

Ask for care instructions before the bill

Request the seller’s washing, drying and ironing guidance for the specific product. Clarify whether the item is prewashed, whether colour transfer is expected and how alterations affect an exchange. Avoid applying a generic care rule to every fibre, dye and finish sold under the same broad category. If you are buying a gift, include the care note and confirm the size-exchange arrangement in writing. Keep the invoice and the seller’s public contact details together so a later question can be traced to the right product.

Understand what your payment supports

The October 2026 ministry release connects khadi production with artisan work and rural livelihoods. That is sector-level context; it does not tell you how the price of a particular garment is shared. Ask a seller who can answer about the producing organisation, the location of the work and the steps completed there. If you feature a maker, use their own verified description of their role. Do not imply that buying one product guarantees a particular wage outcome or proves every environmental claim made about it.

A five-line note to keep on your phone

Before paying, complete a short record: product and intended use; fibre and process information supplied; measurements or fabric width; care and shrinkage guidance; final price and exchange terms. Fill the note with answers, not assumptions. If the story behind the cloth interests you, ask permission to share it and credit the person who explained it. Beyond Soch’s Vocal for Local approach works best when curiosity leads to a purchase that suits you and a description that represents the maker fairly.
