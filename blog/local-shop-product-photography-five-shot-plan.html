Five Product Photos a Local Shop Can Prepare Before Its Next Promotion

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 20 October 2026

Start with unanswered customer questions

A beautiful promotional image is only part of a useful product set. Before the next reel or catalogue update, ask the shop team which questions customers repeatedly send after seeing a photograph. Are they unsure what is included, which size is shown or whether the box is part of the price? Etsy’s photography checklist recommends planning different kinds of shots, while Shopify’s guide includes detail and packaging views. The five-question plan below is Beyond Soch’s suggested working brief for a local shop, not a platform requirement or a promise of sales.

Show the complete offer before styling the scene

For a hypothetical gift set, arrange the exact items that belong to one purchase and count them against the product description. Keep a separate note of props and optional extras. If a decorative tray is not included, the caption should make that clear rather than allowing the viewer to infer a larger offer. Have the person responsible for the product confirm this frame before continuing. That one check can prevent a creator from publishing an attractive arrangement that the shop cannot actually supply.

Five-photo planning checklist

This is an editorial planning example, not a marketplace requirement.

PhotoCustomer question
The complete offerWhat exactly is included in this purchase?
A useful size referenceWill it fit the space or purpose I have in mind?
One decision-making detailWhat feature do I need to inspect closely?
Clearly identified variantsWhich colour, size or version am I selecting?
The actual packingWhat will arrive, and which decoration is optional?

Choose a size reference and a meaningful detail

Decide which measurement affects the buying decision and record it accurately in the description. A contextual image can help, but it should not replace dimensions or make an item appear larger through a misleading angle. For the detail frame, pick something a customer genuinely needs to inspect: a closure, a finish, a compartment or a readable label. Use the real item being offered, not a more elaborate sample. Ask whether the image answers a question before adding another near-identical close-up.

Keep variants and packing traceable

Photograph each version you intend to advertise and connect the image to the correct product name or code. Do not recolour one photograph and present it as documentation of a different physical item. Photograph the packing that accompanies the actual offer; keep a styled celebration setup separate from a routine dispatch example. If the business changes the contents or box later, mark the old set for review. A simple file name with a product code and date is easier to trace than a folder full of final-final images.

Make a small setup repeatable

Use a stable surface, keep the product in focus and compare the image with the item under the light in which you will approve it. Etsy advises against filters that alter product colours; Shopify discusses keeping a camera stable and compressing photographs for online use. Keep a clean original and export copies suited to the actual channel. Before posting, inspect a phone-sized preview: can the buyer still identify the selected version, read the necessary detail and distinguish the product from decorative props?

Hand the set to a creator with one clear approval route

Share the five selected frames, the product facts, the intended use of each image and the person who can approve factual corrections. Agree on where the photographs may be reused rather than assuming one shoot covers every advertisement. If the promotion includes a paid or gifted creator feature, plan an appropriate disclosure separately. This guide does not describe an existing client campaign. Its aim is a smaller, more useful image brief: enough evidence for a customer to understand the offer, without relying on an unsupported claim that better photographs guarantee orders.
