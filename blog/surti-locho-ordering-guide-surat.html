Surti Locho: How to Order and Enjoy Surat’s Soft Steamed Farsan

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 29 October 2026

A Surti snack with a soft centre

The Surat district administration describes locho as a steamed snack made from gram flour and lentils that is best eaten immediately. Its loose, uneven texture is the key difference many first-time eaters notice. Rather than neat cubes, it is commonly served soft enough to eat with a spoon. Recipes and toppings vary by maker, so treat this guide as an ordering framework rather than a claim that one version represents every kitchen in Surat.

Locho and khaman are different textures

Both belong to the wider family of steamed gram-flour farsan, but khaman usually sets into a more defined, spongy piece while locho stays softer and less uniform. That difference changes how chutney and toppings sit on the food. If you are tasting both, try a plain or lightly dressed version first so you can compare texture before adding stronger flavours. Do not rely on appearance alone to identify ingredients or preparation methods.

Ask how the toppings are served

A serving may include sev, chutney, spices, coriander, onion, oil or butter, but the combination depends on the vendor and the customer’s choice. Ask which toppings are included, whether extra chilli or butter can be left out, and whether the chutney is mild or spicy. If you prefer to control texture, ask whether toppings can be supplied separately. Add them gradually: once a soft steamed snack is covered in liquid or oil, its texture changes quickly.

Make dietary questions specific

Gram flour and lentils are central to many locho preparations, but a particular recipe may include other ingredients or be handled alongside foods you avoid. Ask about oil, butter, dairy, chutney ingredients and spice blends when they matter to you. For an allergy, explain the specific allergen and ask about shared equipment and preparation surfaces; appearance cannot confirm safety. If the vendor cannot confirm what you need, choose a different option rather than taking a risk.

Eat fresh and plan takeaway carefully

Locho is valued for its just-steamed texture, and the Surat district’s description recommends eating it immediately. For takeaway, ask the seller how best to transport that preparation and whether toppings can be packed separately. Do not assume a universal shelf life or leave cooked food at room temperature for extended periods. Follow the vendor’s handling advice and standard food-safety practice, and consume it promptly. A dish that travels poorly should not be presented as if its texture will remain unchanged.

Feature the food without inventing a story

A useful food reel can show the texture, explain the difference from khaman and identify toppings only after checking them with the seller. Get permission before filming staff or a kitchen, confirm the spelling of the business name and disclose any paid collaboration. Do not repeat a popular origin story as established fact unless a reliable source supports it. Local food culture is already interesting without invented secret recipes, exaggerated claims or unsupported “best in town” labels.
