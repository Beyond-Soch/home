ONDC for Local Shops: A Readiness Checklist Before You Sign Up

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 29 October 2026

Understand what ONDC is

The Open Network for Digital Commerce describes itself as digital-commerce infrastructure, not a single shopping app. Buyer applications and seller-side participants connect through network standards, allowing participating sellers to become discoverable across compatible buyer apps. For a local shop, the practical question is not simply “How do I download ONDC?” but which seller application or Seller Network Participant will onboard the business, maintain the catalogue and support fulfilment. Read the official role descriptions before choosing a provider.

Check the onboarding route and provider

ONDC’s official guidance explains that Seller Network Participants connect sellers to the network, digitise catalogues, support payment dispersal and help sellers follow e-commerce practices. Ask a prospective provider which services it supplies, which tasks remain yours and where support requests go. Confirm the provider’s identity through official ONDC information. Do not assume that a message using the ONDC name is authorised, and do not hand over passwords, one-time codes or banking access to an unverified caller.

Prepare the product catalogue first

Create a clean product list with accurate names, descriptions, variants, sizes, stock levels, prices, taxes where applicable and clear photographs that you own or have permission to use. Check that the catalogue matches the products you can reliably fulfil. For packaged or regulated goods, verify the labelling and licence requirements that apply to your category. A digital listing does not replace legal compliance. If inventory changes every day, decide how stock will be updated before accepting online orders.

Calculate the complete operating cost

Ask for every onboarding charge, subscription, commission, payment or settlement deduction, logistics cost, packaging expense, return charge and cancellation rule in writing. Confirm who handles customer support and disputes, how settlements are reconciled and how much notice is needed to exit. ONDC’s public pages describe potential network benefits, but no listing guarantees orders, a specific fee saving or a profitable channel for every business. Compare the provider’s actual offer with existing sales channels and margins before committing.

Run a small pilot and measure results

Start with a manageable set of products rather than uploading the entire shop on day one. Test the full process: order notification, stock confirmation, packing, pickup, delivery updates, cancellation, return and settlement reconciliation. Keep a simple sheet for orders, gross sales, deductions, delivery failures, returns, support time and repeat purchases. Review results after a defined trial period. If a product is often unavailable or expensive to ship, fix the operational problem before expanding the catalogue.

Use a cautious sign-up checklist

Before agreeing, confirm the seller app or participant, required business documents, category eligibility, catalogue responsibilities, service levels, fees, settlement schedule, returns, data access and exit terms. Keep copies of signed terms and payment receipts. Be sceptical of guaranteed sales, pressure to pay immediately or unofficial registration links. The best route for one retailer may not suit another, so use official ONDC material to compare options and seek independent accounting or legal advice when contract or tax treatment is unclear.
