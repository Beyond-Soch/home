WhatsApp Business Catalog: A Practical Setup Guide for Local Shops

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 25 October 2026

What the catalog is for

WhatsApp says businesses using the WhatsApp Business app can create a catalog that customers can browse and share. For a small shop, this can reduce repeated questions and give every enquiry a consistent starting point. It is not a replacement for stock control, payment records or a complete website, and it should not promise instant availability. Think of it as a compact mobile menu of products or services. The catalog works best when each listing helps a customer answer four questions quickly: what is it, what does it cost, is it suitable, and how do I ask or order?

Prepare information before uploading

Create a simple sheet with product name, current price, short description, size or variant, material or key specification, product code and website link where relevant. WhatsApp’s official setup guidance lists the name as required and allows optional price, sale price, description, website link and code; it also asks for country of origin in the current catalog flow. Confirm every fact before publishing. Do not use ‘handmade’, ‘organic’, ‘original’ or ‘made in Gujarat’ as decorative keywords unless you can support the claim for that item.

Use photographs that answer buying questions

Choose a clean main image, then add views that show scale, texture, back or side, packaging and a meaningful detail. Keep colour editing restrained so the delivered item resembles the listing. WhatsApp’s help page currently says an item can include up to ten photos or videos and that uploaded media is reviewed against its business messaging policy. The limit or interface can change, so check the app rather than building your entire workflow around an old tutorial. Use only images you own or have permission to publish.

Write scan-friendly listings

Lead with the specific product or service name, not a string of promotional adjectives. Put the most decision-relevant detail in the first sentence: material, portion, duration, compatibility or intended use. Explain what the price includes and clearly label optional extras. For food, cosmetics and other regulated or safety-sensitive categories, include only verified information and follow applicable rules. Avoid medical promises and artificial urgency. A customer reading on a small screen should not have to message just to discover the basic variant or whether the listing is still current.

Organise and share with intention

WhatsApp supports catalog collections, which can group items into useful customer paths such as new arrivals, services, festive gifts or products under a genuine price band. Keep categories few and understandable. Share a direct item or catalog link only when it answers the conversation; sending the entire catalog after every message can feel like spam. Use labels or lists to track enquiries and quick replies for recurring questions, but review automated language so it still sounds human. Obtain consent and follow messaging rules before sending promotions.

Adopt a weekly maintenance routine

Once a week, hide unavailable products, remove expired offers, correct prices, test links and replace weak images. WhatsApp notes that catalog items become visible after approval and that rejected items can be reviewed through the app. Keep a separate master record so an app issue does not erase your product information. Check which listings trigger real, qualified enquiries rather than judging only by views. For a local business reel, link to one relevant collection and measure messages that mention it. A small accurate catalog usually builds more trust than a large neglected one.
