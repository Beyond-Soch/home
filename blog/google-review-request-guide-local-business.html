Google Review Requests: A Policy-Safe Guide for Local Businesses

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 24 October 2026

Use Google’s own request link

Google Business Profile Help allows businesses to create and share a review link or QR code. The current instructions direct an authorised manager to the Business Profile, then to Read Reviews and Get more reviews. Google notes that the QR code is generated on a computer browser rather than a mobile device. Download and test the code before printing it. A direct official link is safer than a third-party form that may collect customer data or redirect people unpredictably.

Ask for an honest review, not a high rating

Use neutral wording such as: If you used our service, you can share an honest Google review here. Do not ask only happy customers, script a five-star response or tell staff to pressure people at checkout. Google’s Maps policies require reviews to reflect a genuine experience and prohibit fake or biased engagement. A valid request makes feedback easy while leaving the customer free to review, decline and choose the rating and wording.

Never exchange value for a review

Google explicitly prohibits incentives such as payments, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for posting, revising or removing a review. That means no free drink for five stars, no coupon after a screenshot and no refund offered only if a negative review disappears. A general loyalty benefit that is unrelated to a review is a different programme, but the review request itself must not be tied to a reward. Keep staff instructions written and unambiguous.

Place the link at natural touchpoints

Google suggests receipts, thank-you emails, the end of a chat interaction and an in-store QR display. Choose moments after a real transaction or completed service, not before delivery. Keep the card or message short and identify the correct business location. For multi-location brands, generate and label a separate link for each profile so reviews do not land on the wrong branch. Do not expose the link in spam messages to people who were never customers.

Protect privacy in follow-up

A review is public, so do not ask customers to include order numbers, phone numbers, medical details, addresses or other sensitive information. If a service problem needs investigation, move the conversation to an appropriate private channel. When replying publicly, acknowledge the feedback without confirming private facts. A review QR code should open Google directly; it should not secretly capture contact data before forwarding the user.

Respond without arguing or revealing details

Google recommends useful and positive replies to show responsiveness. Thank the reviewer, address the issue at a high level and offer a private contact route when more information is needed. Do not publish internal records to prove the customer wrong. If a review violates policy, use Google’s reporting tools rather than organising counter-reviews. A critical but genuine experience is not automatically removable, and a calm response often tells future customers more than a defensive debate.

Create a simple monthly audit

Once a month, test the link and QR code, confirm that it points to the correct profile, review staff scripts and sample recent replies. Track the number of requests sent and reviews received without rewarding individual employees for five-star outcomes. Save the official policy links and recheck them because platform rules can change. A trustworthy review programme is deliberately boring: genuine customers, neutral invitations, no incentives, privacy-aware replies and a clear record of who manages the profile.
