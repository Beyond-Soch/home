Google Business Profile Photos: A Practical Plan for Local Businesses

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 22 October 2026

Treat the profile as customer orientation

Google’s Business Profile guidance says category-specific photos help customers understand the features they use when choosing a business. Start with the questions a first-time visitor has: What does the entrance look like? What is the inside like? What products or services are actually offered? Who provides them? Build the shot list around those decisions rather than uploading a folder of near-identical promotional graphics. A verified profile is required for business-added photos to show, so confirm ownership and access before planning a large shoot.

Follow the current technical baseline

Google currently recommends JPG or PNG images between 10 KB and 5 MB, with a recommended 720-by-720-pixel resolution and a 250-by-250-pixel minimum. The guidance also asks for images that are in focus, well lit and free from significant alterations, excessive filters or AI changes so they represent reality. Export a clean master, check file size and keep an original copy. Specifications can change, so recheck the linked official page before building a permanent production template.

Capture the outside and approach

For a storefront, photograph the exterior from the directions customers commonly approach. Include permanent signage and enough street context to make the entrance recognisable, but avoid exposing private information or creating a traffic hazard. Make day and evening versions only if both reflect real customer conditions. Do not remove neighbouring buildings, add fictional parking or brighten a closed entrance until it looks open. The image should reduce arrival uncertainty, not create an idealised location.

Show interior, products and service honestly

Google suggests interior, product, at-work, food-and-drink and team categories where relevant. Choose the categories that match the business rather than forcing every type. Use representative products, real service areas and the current team with permission. Remove clutter that would not normally be present, but do not stage facilities the business does not offer. For food, photograph the portion and presentation customers actually receive; for services, avoid showing customer faces, records or confidential screens.

Create a small complete set first

A practical first upload can include three exterior views, three interior views, three representative products or services and three team or at-work images where consent allows. This mirrors Google’s category tips without turning the numbers into a ranking promise. Add a logo and cover image separately if the profile supports them. Name and archive each original by category and capture date, then review the live profile to ensure crops have not hidden the sign, product or main subject.

Reuse assets without making every platform identical

A truthful Business Profile photo can also inform a reel, website or directory listing, but each format has different crops and context. Keep a horizontal or square documentary master for search and create a separate vertical edit for social media. Do not overlay phone numbers or promotional claims onto every photo; Google’s policies govern posted content and may change. When a time-limited offer is involved, use the appropriate post type and current terms rather than turning an old discount graphic into evergreen location imagery.

Maintain the set after publishing

Review the profile when signage, décor, menu, team or access changes. Replace misleading owner photos instead of letting outdated images remain the primary customer reference. Check that newly uploaded files are visible, correctly oriented and attached to the right location. Customer-uploaded photos are separate from the owner’s set, so do not claim control over every image shown. A quarterly visual audit gives a homegrown business a manageable routine: verify reality, retire outdated assets and add only the views that answer a real customer question.
