Sankheda Furniture: A Buyer’s Guide to Gujarat’s Lacquered Woodcraft

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 26 October 2026

What identifies the craft

Sankheda furniture is associated with Sankheda in Chhota Udaipur district. The Government of India’s One District One Product portal lists it as a GI-tagged product of the district, while Gujarat Tourism describes colourful pieces made by craftspeople from the Kharadi community. Characteristic furniture includes turned wooden components, painted floral or geometric decoration and a polished lacquer finish. The visual vocabulary may appear on chairs, tables, swings and other forms. A buyer should connect the name to place, process and maker—not to colour alone.

Understand the making sequence

Gujarat Tourism explains that teakwood is shaped on a lathe and chiselled before motifs are painted and the surface is furnished. Its craft documentation also describes lacquer work and the use of an agate stone in finishing. Contemporary workshops may adapt materials, colours and production tools, so ask how a particular piece was made. Do not insist that every modern item match a single historical recipe. What matters is a transparent account of wood, turning, decoration, finish and workshop origin.

Questions before paying

Ask who made the piece, where the workshop is located, which wood and finish were used, and whether components are solid, joined or replaceable. For seating, check stability on a level floor, joint movement and the comfort of the dimensions. For a swing, ask a qualified installer to assess the ceiling, hardware and load requirements; decorative appearance is not structural certification. Request the final size, packed size, weight, care instructions, invoice and any written warranty. A social-media caption is not a substitute for those records.

Inspect paint and construction

View the piece in even light and inspect both prominent and hidden areas. Look at the consistency of turned shapes, smoothness around joints, finish at edges and contact points, and whether the item stands without rocking. Handwork can include small variations, but variation alone does not prove authenticity or excuse a functional defect. Ask before assuming a metallic-looking line is real gold or silver. If provenance or GI status is important, request traceable producer information and verify it rather than relying only on a printed tag.

Plan delivery and placement

Measure doorways, lifts, stair turns and the final space before ordering. Confirm who is responsible for packing, transit damage, assembly and installation. Keep finished wood away from persistent moisture, harsh sunlight and heat unless the maker gives different guidance. Use a soft dry cloth for routine dusting and follow the workshop’s advice before applying polish or cleaner; an unsuitable chemical can alter lacquer or painted details. For a heavy or suspended piece, professional installation and periodic hardware checks are part of ownership.

Promote the maker, not only the object

A strong craft story shows the complete furniture piece, one turned component, a painted motif, a join and the finish under changing light. Film a maker or workshop only with clear permission and identify the artisan, business and location accurately. Do not imply a workshop visit or commission that did not occur. Disclose gifts and paid promotions, and avoid calling an item ‘antique’ without documented age. Vocal for Local becomes meaningful when content helps customers find traceable makers and understand why skilled labour, transport and care matter.
