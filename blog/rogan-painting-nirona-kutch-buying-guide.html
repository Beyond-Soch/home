Rogan Painting in Nirona: A Buyer’s Guide to Kutch’s Oil-Based Art

By Beyond Soch Editorial · 29 October 2026

A distinctive craft associated with Nirona

Gujarat Tourism identifies Nirona, in Kutch, with Rogan painting as well as other craft traditions. The technique decorates cloth with a thick, oil-based colour material and flowing geometric or floral designs. It is specialised handwork, not simply a printed pattern. Treat the village and the makers as the centre of the story: a design has meaning because of the skill, time and knowledge behind it, not only because it looks striking in a display.

How paint and tool work together

Gujarat Tourism describes a process in which castor oil is heated for an extended period and cooled to form a sticky material that is then combined with pigments. The source also describes artisans using a small metal stick to guide the material across cloth. Individual workshops may use different materials or refinements, so ask the maker about the specific piece rather than assuming every object follows one identical formula. Fine lines and controlled flow are part of what distinguishes the technique.

Read the surface closely

Look at how a motif is built: lines may form floral, geometric or tree-like arrangements, with colour and spacing doing much of the visual work. A product photograph can hide scale and surface detail, so ask for a clear image of the complete piece and a close view of the painted area if buying remotely. Do not assume a familiar motif proves origin or authorship. Provenance needs to come from the seller or maker rather than visual guesswork.

Questions worth asking before buying

Ask who made the piece, where the work was completed, what the base cloth and colour materials are, and which parts were hand-painted. Confirm dimensions, intended use, mounting requirements and whether a maker card or receipt is available. If a seller describes an item as original, handmade or made in Nirona, ask what information supports that description. There is no single price that proves authenticity; compare the actual work, materials, scale and provenance instead of using a bargain as the only test.

Care should follow the maker’s advice

Do not assume a painted textile can be washed, ironed directly or exposed to strong sunlight. Ask the maker how to store or display that particular piece and whether it needs a backing, frame or specialist cleaning. Until you have instructions, keep it dry, avoid rubbing the painted surface and protect it from pressure or sharp folds. For a valuable or damaged work, seek advice from a textile conservator instead of testing a household cleaner on the design.

Support the maker and credit the work

If you visit Nirona, check access and workshop arrangements in advance rather than assuming a studio is open to walk-ins. Ask permission before photographing tools, unfinished work or people. When posting a purchase, name the maker only with their consent, credit the craft accurately and do not copy a distinctive design for commercial use without permission. A responsible Vocal for Local story explains the process while letting artisans decide how their work, name and image are represented.
